# General Insurance Capital & Risk Modelling
## Business Brief

### Anthony Utulu Analytics

## 1. Client Scenario

A fictional general insurer has experienced continued portfolio growth and wants to strengthen its understanding of underwriting risk and capital adequacy.

Senior management recognises that average historical claims experience alone does not fully capture the financial consequences of uncertainty, particularly the potential impact of infrequent but severe claims.

Management therefore requires an actuarial risk modelling framework that moves beyond expected losses and evaluates the potential financial impact of adverse claims experience.

---

## 2. Business Problem

The insurer needs to understand the potential distribution of annual insurance losses and determine the level of financial protection that may be required against unexpected claims experience.

In particular, management wants to understand:

- the expected level of annual insurance losses;
- the volatility surrounding expected losses;
- the potential impact of extreme claims;
- the amount of risk capital indicated at different confidence levels;
- the portfolio segments contributing most strongly to insurance risk;
- the potential benefit of diversification; and
- how adverse frequency and severity scenarios could affect the insurer's risk position.

The analysis should translate these findings into information that can support practical management decisions.

---

## 3. Consulting Assignment

Anthony Utulu Analytics has been engaged to develop an actuarial capital and risk modelling framework using historical motor third-party liability insurance experience as the empirical basis of the analysis.

The engagement will combine observed insurance data, statistical modelling and Monte Carlo simulation to evaluate both expected and unexpected insurance losses.

The project will distinguish clearly between:

- observed historical data;
- statistically estimated parameters;
- actuarial modelling assumptions;
- simulated outcomes; and
- management stress scenarios.

---

## 4. Key Business Questions

The analysis will address the following questions:

1. What level of claims should the insurer expect under normal portfolio experience?
2. How volatile could annual claims experience become?
3. Which factors materially influence claim frequency?
4. Which factors influence claim severity?
5. What does the extreme-loss tail of the claims distribution look like?
6. What losses could arise at the 95%, 99% and 99.5% confidence levels?
7. What additional information does Tail Value at Risk (TVaR) provide beyond Value at Risk (VaR)?
8. Which areas of the portfolio contribute disproportionately to overall insurance risk?
9. What diversification or concentration effects exist within the modelled portfolio?
10. How would adverse changes in claim frequency or severity affect the insurer's risk position?
11. What management actions should be considered in response to the findings?

---

## 5. Decisions the Analysis Will Support

The project is intended to provide analytical support for:

- capital planning;
- underwriting and portfolio management;
- risk appetite discussions;
- identification of risk concentrations;
- reinsurance considerations;
- stress and scenario testing; and
- management monitoring of insurance risk.

The purpose of the analysis is not simply to produce a statistical model, but to improve understanding of the decisions that management may need to make.

---

## 6. Scope of the Engagement

The empirical analysis is based on motor third-party liability insurance data.

The project will model:

- claim frequency;
- claim severity;
- aggregate insurance losses;
- extreme-loss behaviour;
- selected risk measures;
- modelled risk capital;
- portfolio risk concentration and diversification; and
- adverse frequency and severity scenarios.

---

## 7. Scope Boundary

This project is an actuarial portfolio case study focused primarily on motor insurance underwriting risk.

It is not intended to reproduce the complete regulatory capital framework of a real insurer.

In particular, the project does not calculate or claim to calculate a regulatory Solvency Capital Requirement (SCR). A complete regulatory capital assessment would require additional risk categories, dependencies, regulatory specifications and company-specific information that are outside the scope of the available dataset and this case study.

Any capital figures produced by the project will therefore be described as modelled or economic risk capital within the defined analytical framework.

---

## 8. Expected Deliverables

The engagement is expected to produce:

- a documented data-quality assessment;
- targeted exploratory analysis;
- claim frequency modelling;
- claim severity and tail-risk modelling;
- aggregate-loss simulation;
- VaR and TVaR estimates at selected confidence levels;
- analysis of risk concentration and diversification;
- stress and scenario testing;

---

## 9. Project Standard

This engagement will follow the Anthony Utulu Analytics principle:

> **Simple visuals. Sophisticated analysis.**

Every analytical step should contribute to answering a defined actuarial or business question. Results will be interpreted in terms of their implications for risk and management decision-making rather than presented as statistical outputs alone.
