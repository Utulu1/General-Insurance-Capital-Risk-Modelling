# Actuarial Analysis Framework
## General Insurance Capital & Risk Modelling

### Anthony Utulu Analytics

## 1. Purpose

This framework defines the actuarial questions, modelling approach and
decision objectives for the General Insurance Capital & Risk Modelling
engagement.

The purpose is to ensure that each analytical stage contributes directly
to understanding the insurer's underwriting risk and the potential
financial impact of unexpected claims experience.

The analysis follows the principle:

> Simple visuals. Sophisticated analysis.

---

## 2. Analytical Objective

The central actuarial objective is to estimate the distribution of
aggregate insurance losses and use that distribution to evaluate expected
losses, unexpected losses, tail risk and modelled risk capital.

The framework separates the analysis into:

1. Frequency risk
2. Severity risk
3. Tail risk
4. Aggregate loss risk
5. Capital risk
6. Risk concentration and diversification
7. Stress and scenario risk
8. Management interpretation

---

## 3. Frequency Risk

### Business Question

How frequently should claims be expected to occur, and which portfolio
characteristics materially influence claim frequency?

### Analysis

The analysis will:

- examine observed claim frequency relative to exposure;
- investigate variation across relevant policy and risk characteristics;
- assess the distribution of claim counts;
- develop an appropriate frequency model;
- compare alternative specifications where justified; and
- validate the selected model.

### Management Relevance

Understanding frequency risk can support underwriting, portfolio monitoring,
risk segmentation and assessment of how changes in claim incidence could
affect aggregate losses.

---

## 4. Severity Risk

### Business Question

When a claim occurs, how large could the financial loss be?

### Analysis

The analysis will:

- examine the empirical distribution of individual claim amounts;
- assess skewness and variability;
- investigate relevant risk characteristics where supported by the data;
- evaluate suitable severity distributions;
- estimate model parameters; and
- assess goodness of fit and model limitations.

### Management Relevance

Severity modelling helps management understand the financial consequences
of claims and provides an essential component of aggregate loss modelling.

---

## 5. Tail Risk

### Business Question

How significant are infrequent but exceptionally large claims?

### Analysis

The analysis will:

- investigate the upper tail of the severity distribution;
- assess extreme claim observations;
- compare empirical and modelled tail behaviour;
- consider whether additional tail modelling is justified; and
- evaluate the sensitivity of capital results to severe losses.

Extreme observations will not be removed merely because they are unusual.

### Management Relevance

Tail risk is particularly important for capital planning, risk appetite,
reinsurance considerations and stress testing.

---

## 6. Aggregate Loss Risk

### Business Question

What range of total insurance losses could the portfolio experience over
a one-year period?

### Analysis

Frequency and severity models will be combined using Monte Carlo simulation
to generate a distribution of aggregate annual losses.

The simulation will be used to estimate:

- expected aggregate loss;
- aggregate loss volatility;
- selected percentiles of the loss distribution; and
- the shape and behaviour of the adverse-loss tail.

### Management Relevance

The aggregate loss distribution provides the bridge between individual
claims experience and portfolio-level financial risk.

---

## 7. Capital Risk Measures

### Business Question

How much financial protection may be required against unexpected insurance
losses?

### Analysis

The project will evaluate risk measures including:

- Value at Risk (VaR) at 95%;
- Value at Risk (VaR) at 99%;
- Value at Risk (VaR) at 99.5%; and
- Tail Value at Risk (TVaR), where appropriate.

Unexpected loss will be evaluated relative to expected aggregate loss.

### Important Interpretation

The resulting figures represent modelled or economic risk capital within
the scope of this case study.

They must not be described as a regulatory Solvency Capital Requirement
(SCR).

---

## 8. Risk Concentration and Diversification

### Business Question

Which components of the portfolio contribute most strongly to overall risk,
and what diversification or concentration effects exist?

### Analysis

Where supported by the available data and model structure, the project will:

- identify material risk segments;
- compare segment-level loss behaviour;
- assess contributions to aggregate risk;
- evaluate concentration effects; and
- quantify diversification effects where methodologically justified.

No diversification benefit will be assumed without an explicit modelling
basis.

### Management Relevance

This analysis can help management identify where risk is concentrated and
where portfolio structure materially affects capital requirements.

---

## 9. Stress and Scenario Testing

### Business Question

How vulnerable is the portfolio if claims experience becomes worse than
expected?

### Analysis

Selected adverse scenarios will be applied to the model, potentially
including:

- higher claim frequency;
- higher average claim severity;
- deterioration in both frequency and severity; and
- increased exposure to large claims.

Scenario assumptions will be stated explicitly and kept separate from
observed historical experience.

### Management Relevance

Stress testing provides management with an indication of how the insurer's
risk position may change under adverse but plausible conditions.

---

## 10. Model Validation

Model selection will not be based solely on producing the most sophisticated
statistical specification.

Models will be assessed using appropriate evidence such as:

- empirical fit;
- residual behaviour;
- goodness-of-fit diagnostics;
- stability;
- tail behaviour;
- actuarial interpretability; and
- suitability for the intended capital application.

Where limitations remain, they will be documented rather than hidden.

---

## 11. Management Interpretation

The final analysis must move beyond statistical results.

For each material finding, the project will consider:

**What happened?**

**Why does it matter?**

**What does it mean for portfolio risk?**

**What decision could management consider?**

Potential management implications may relate to:

- underwriting;
- portfolio monitoring;
- capital planning;
- risk appetite;
- risk concentration;
- reinsurance; and
- stress testing.

Recommendations will remain proportionate to what the data and modelling
evidence actually support.

---

## 12. Evidence Hierarchy

Throughout the project, results will be clearly classified as:

Observed Data
↓
Estimated Parameters
↓
Actuarial Assumptions
↓
Simulated Outcomes
↓
Management Scenarios

These categories will not be presented interchangeably.
